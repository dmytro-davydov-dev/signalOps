## What it does

Ingests structured logs, trace summaries, and deployment events through an authenticated batch API; normalizes them through Kafka into PostgreSQL with receipts and a transactional outbox; detects incidents with durable time windows; maintains a temporal service graph; and investigates each incident with a durable, checkpointed, budgeted workflow: one tool-using agent (`v2`) or coordinator, three specialists, synthesizer, and verifier (`v3`). Diagnoses cite evidence that is checked against workspace, replay scope, and evidence cutoff, or abstain as insufficient evidence. A React workspace shows incidents, evidence, the graph, live progress over SSE, and diagnoses. An evaluation lab replays held-out scenarios in isolated scopes and compares deterministic, single-agent, and multi-agent variants under equal limits. Prometheus metrics, a Grafana dashboard, retention, backup and restore, and rotatable hashed credentials are included.

## Known limits

- **No diagnostic quality claim.** Every recorded investigation and evaluation used the fake provider (`measures_model_quality: false`). Whether agents beat rules, or multi-agent beats single-agent, is untested; on the fake provider `v3` cost twice as much as `v2` for the same accuracy.
- **Synthetic data only.** Scenarios come from the project's generator and the `demo-shop` application.
- **Single-node development environment.** One Kafka broker (replication factor 1), one PostgreSQL without WAL archiving, local volumes, no failover. Recovery is to the last `pg_dump`.
- **Not ready for public exposure.** Shared-secret credentials per workspace without users or SSO; no TLS; Redis, Kafka, and metrics listeners unauthenticated on the private network ([security.md](../security.md#not-covered)).
- **Throughput.** About 160 records/s end to end on the replay, capped by the raw partition key (70% of demo traffic on one partition) and per-record transactions. 100 records/s for 30 minutes is not claimed.
- **Detection quality.** Ten times the demo's healthy traffic produced 15 false inventory error-rate incidents in the replay; not investigated.
- **Operational gaps.** The consumer-group rewind after a restore is documented but not exercised; retention has no scheduler; incidents and cited evidence never expire; no per-source ingestion rate limit (`429`); outage checks are opt-in and not in CI.
- **Exactly-once model execution is not provided.** Calls in flight when a worker dies are recorded as unknown and may be billed again on retry.
- macOS and a cold first image build were not tested by the quick-start check.

## Next priorities

1. **A budgeted paid-model evaluation** of `v2` and `v3` on the held-out split with repeated trials, under an explicit scope and spending limit, to answer whether agents and multi-agent execution earn their cost. Record the results next to the fake-provider run.
2. **Detection false positives under load**: investigate the inventory rule's firings on healthy traffic and add a scenario that pins the fix.
3. **Throughput**: review the raw partition key against replay window ordering, then batch records per transaction in the consumers (both analysed in [benchmarks.md](../benchmarks.md#profile-and-tuning)); re-run the sustained benchmark with the fixed generator.
4. **Exercise the restore-time consumer-group rewind** on a throwaway stack and add it to the backup test.
5. **Public-exposure prerequisites** if the system leaves a laptop: OIDC or an authenticating proxy, TLS, broker and Redis authentication.
6. Deferred backlog unchanged: metrics adapter, dedicated graph store, schema registry, multi-broker Kafka, cloud deployment, additional providers, autonomous remediation ([roadmap](../development_roadmap.md#deferred-backlog)).
